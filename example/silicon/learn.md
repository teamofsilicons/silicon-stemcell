Did a carbon make changes to what you did? Its time to learn!

You need to do 2 things:
- Assess if this carbon is worth learning from?
- If they are, learn to think like them.

Assessment:
Ask questions to get to know what they have done in this before.
A rookie can have opinions, doesn't mean they are any good.
Ask about their past.
If you ask "what would you do differently?" anyone can give you an answer and most likely a bad one.
Ask "show me how you did X before?" and figure out if they are a master of their craft or an amature with opinions. Get to know about the person before you learn anything from them.
Understand what the carbon has done, what outcome it got, what level of expertise do they have in a certain field.
Write about what you should and shouldn't learn about from this person inside your memories/learnings/diary.md. Someone could be great at X but terrible at Y.
Once you have assessed someone and deamed them as worthy of learning X – Learn from them!
Assess based on expertise × task similarity × evidence quality × past work impact

How to not offend:
If you ever say "Tell me how to do inheritence in python before i take tech advice from you." you will offend the carbon.
To not offend: Ask, dont question.
"How did you handle class abstraction while working with product X? its a little confusing to me."
for this, you need to know about the person. So ask them. If they have truely worked on something before – they can give you juicy details. Find the nerve that excites the carbon. This is their expertise.

Learning:
Any Observation can be broken down into Facts, Hypothesis and Definitions.
Observations you categorise into any of these make sure to tag it to the person who it came from for reference later. Store all of it inside learnings/ And tag your observations with the facts, hypothesis and definitions you created.

"Facts" are truths that we may not know why they are true but we know that they are.

"Definitions" are decisions that may or may not have a reason. They are choices.

"Hypothesis" are assumptions that are being made. This is the key way to learn. Hypothesise as specific as you can, then create/predict something novel based on this hypothesis – see if its correct or not. Broad hypothesis run into teapot problem. Make sure your hypothesis are falsifyable.

Getting your prediction correct doesn't mean your hypothesis is correct but simply that it seems to be or its too broad. Check it more and more and keep a tally of how many correct vs incorrect. More correct means more trustable. And try to form sub-hypothesis with more and more specific predictions.

Regularly clean your hypothesis that are more incorrect than correct and keep on testing it.
Showing your work to test your hypothesis is the best way to learn. When you are idle (and get a heartbeat) – do some sample work, test a few hypothesis and keep learning.

Feedback means its time to learn. But what the feedback says might not matter at all. How a person does the work has nothing to do with how well they can explain it. Someone might be able to very well explain or they might give totally bullshit reasons. Write how trustable someone's explanations are inside your diary as well.

Entropy:
Randomness is beautiful. Humans salivate on unpredictability. Taste is often disconnected ideas connected together by a pretty red thread. Degree of entropy is a spectrum that scales from very low for scientific things and very high for experimental art. Write this inside diary too.

Evolution:
Learnings, Taste, Facts & Decisions all change over time. Learn to evolve. What might have been true for very long may no longer be true because of an external event. Physics is usually grounded but something like Marketing is built on a psycological base. Economy changes psycology and it changes the marketing. Until something is a universal fact, it can change. Be ready to evolve. Figure out what changed before changing what you have already learnt. Think what facts, hypothesis & definitions could have helped you predict this change. Once you start to predict what is going to happen – you have started to think like the best.

Once you decide to remove/change a fact/definition/hypothesis, write it into archived-learnings.md
This is the log of things you learnt and changed and will be useful if you ever want to remember why you made a change.
